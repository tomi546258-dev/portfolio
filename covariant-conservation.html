Covariant conservation,
metric volume forms,
and symmetry-adapted spectral bases

Bódis Tamás · ORCID 0009-0004-6349-712X · Manuscript GM-D-25-00033 · Handling editor: Arieh Iserles
Abstract
We establish a covariant conservation identity for the canonical divergence of a vector density built from the metric volume form $\sqrt{|g|}$, and show how this identity constrains the spectral decomposition of geometric differential operators on curved domains. Applying the framework to a helical tube geometry, we prove that the first-order perturbative correction to the ground-state eigenvalue vanishes identically ($\lambda^{(1)} = 0$) as a direct consequence of the conservation law, and derive selection rules governing inter-mode coupling. Second-order corrections are expressed via Lommel-type Bessel integrals, yielding closed-form perturbative spectra not previously reported in the literature.
Key results
Theorem 1
Covariant conservation identity
The canonical divergence of $\sqrt{|g|}\,V^\mu$ satisfies an exact identity that is metric-compatible and coordinate-independent. This extends the Piola–Nanson formula to curved spectral problems.
Theorem 2 — Helical tube
Vanishing first-order correction
For the helical tube perturbation, $\lambda^{(1)} = 0$ follows directly from the conservation law — no first-order energy shift occurs. This is a symmetry consequence, not a coincidence.
Selection rule
Coupling rule $\Delta m = \pm 1,\; \Delta n = 0$
Only modes with azimuthal index differing by exactly one are coupled by the helical perturbation. Axial quantum number $n$ is conserved across the coupling.
New result
Lommel–Bessel second-order spectrum
Second-order corrections $\lambda^{(2)}$ reduce to Lommel-type integrals of Bessel functions, giving a closed-form perturbative spectrum not previously reported in the geometric mechanics literature.
Central identity
Covariant conservation law
$$\nabla_\mu \!\left(\sqrt{|g|}\;V^\mu\right) = \sqrt{|g|}\;\nabla_\mu V^\mu + V^\mu\,\partial_\mu\!\sqrt{|g|}$$
